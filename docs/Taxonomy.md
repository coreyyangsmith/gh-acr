| Cluster                            | Dichotomy (LHS vs RHS)                                                | Key Single-Agent Challenges (Relevance)                                                                                         | Key Multi-Agent Challenges (Relevance)                                                                                                          |
|------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Roles, Goals & Coordination        | Fine-grained roles vs. coarse-grained roles                           | **Coarse-grained (big agent):** context overflow, prompt placement bias, weak decomposition, hard-to-interpret monolithic behavior. | **Fine-grained (many agents):** role overlap, coordination overhead, goal misalignment, cascading failures, higher cost & latency.             |
| Roles, Goals & Coordination        | Central orchestration vs. decentralized coordination                  | **Central (single orchestrator agent):** bottleneck risk, still subject to SA bias & non-determinism.                             | **Decentralized (many peers):** protocol brittleness, schema drift, fragmented decisions, harder traceability & accountability.               |
| Context & Knowledge Management     | Global shared state vs. local partial views                           | **Global (single agent with broad context):** context window limits, primacy/recency bias, “needle in a haystack” issues.        | **Local (per-agent views):** fragmented system state, inconsistent beliefs about repo/branch, incompatible patches, compounding misunderstandings. |
| Context & Knowledge Management     | Full-fidelity context vs. compressed intermediates                    | **Full-fidelity:** overload, placement bias, slow inference; model fixates on salient but non-critical info.                     | **Compressed (summaries):** lossy compression, summarizer bias, divergent summaries across agents, downstream decisions built on partial views.|
| Epistemic Quality, Bias & Drift    | Single-model homogeneity vs. multi-model heterogeneity                | **Single-model:** systemic training/data bias, self-preference for its own outputs, opaque epistemic status of answers.          | **Multi-model:** arbitration between disagreeing models, cross-model bias (favoring one vendor), more complex compounding/interaction of biases. |
| Epistemic Quality, Bias & Drift    | Raw outputs vs. normalized representations (paraphrased/standardized) | **Raw outputs:** style, verbosity, and framing bias affect how later prompts (or humans) interpret content.                      | **Normalized:** normalizer’s own bias; possible loss of nuance; “echo chamber” where normalized viewpoints overwrite genuine disagreements.     |
| Assurance, Testing & Observability | Shallow single-step vs. deep multi-stage pipelines                    | **Shallow (single step):** hallucination, non-determinism, limited capacity to handle complex tasks in one shot.                 | **Deep (multi-stage):** exploding test surface (unit/agent/group/system), more emergent failures, cascading errors, harder debugging.          |
| Assurance, Testing & Observability | Lightweight logging vs. full observability                            | **Lightweight:** poor explainability, hard to reconstruct why the agent failed or succeeded.                                     | **Full tracing in MAS:** huge volume of logs, trace complexity, privacy/leakage risk, still no true introspection into model internals.        |
| Human Integration, Security & Cost | Integration (co-pilot) vs. automation (bot)                           | **Integration (SA assistant):** trust calibration, partial automation, users still must validate outputs.                        | **Automation (MAS pipeline):** accountability for bad merges, larger blast radius for failures, organizational resistance to full autonomy.    |
| Human Integration, Security & Cost | Low-autonomy (guardrailed) vs. high-autonomy (free-form actions)      | **Low-autonomy SA:** limited tools/actions, but still vulnerable to prompt injection & subtle bias.                              | **High-autonomy MAS:** enlarged attack surface, tool abuse, secret leakage over many hops, harder to design safe global policies.              |
| Human Integration, Security & Cost | Minimalist single-agent vs. redundant multi-agent for robustness      | **Minimalist SA:** cheaper, faster, but limited redundancy; single point of failure for hallucinations or misaligned objectives. | **Redundant MAS:** voting/critique can catch some errors, but increases cost/latency, may still share systemic bias, creates conflict to resolve.|
